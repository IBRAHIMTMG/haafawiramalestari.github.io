Indonesian Wood & Rice Husk Pellets: Export & Fuel Guide

By IBRAHIM • Tuesday, May 26, 2026

INDONESIAN WOOD & RICE HUSK PELLETS Export & Fuel Compliance Guide

Indonesia has established itself as a significant and growing supplier of wood pellets to Japan, driven by Japan's Feed-in Tariff (FIT) policy framework and its commitment to carbon neutrality by 2050. Exports from Indonesia to Japan increased from approximately 54 metric tons in 2021 to over 52,700 metric tons in 2023 — a trajectory that is expected to continue as Japan pursues its biomass energy targets.

This guide provides Indonesian exporters and their Japanese counterparts with a comprehensive reference covering:

•       Indonesian export regulations and mandatory documentation requirements

•       Japanese import and phytosanitary procedures

•       Sustainability certification schemes recognised by Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI)

•       Japan's FIT/FIP programme eligibility and lifecycle GHG calculation obligations

•       Quality standards applicable to industrial and residential wood pellets

•       Logistics, shipping classifications, and customs codes

 

Note: The Revised Clean Wood Act came into full effect in Japan on 1 April 2025, introducing mandatory legality due diligence obligations for Japanese importers. Indonesian exporters must ensure that all SVLK documentation and chain-of-custody records are in order to support their buyers' compliance.

 

Part 1: Market Overview

1.1 Indonesia's Position in the Japan Wood Pellet Market

Japan and the Republic of Korea collectively account for more than 99% of Indonesia's wood pellet exports, making Northeast Asia the definitive strategic market for Indonesian producers. Key production provinces include Central Java, East Java, and Gorontalo, with primary wood species comprising Sengon, Meranti, and Teak.

1.2 Japan's Policy Environment and Import Drivers

Japan's biomass import demand is primarily policy-driven. The government has committed to reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 46% by fiscal year 2030 (relative to FY2013 levels) and achieving carbon neutrality by 2050. The 2023 Green Transformation (GX) Basic Policy designates biomass as a primary renewable energy source.


Japan's biomass power target for 2030 is 3.7–4.6% of total energy output — equivalent to approximately 13–18 million metric tons of biomass annually. In 2023, Japan imported an estimated 4.25 million bone-dry tons of wood pellets, with demand expected to grow further under existing and committed FIT/FIP projects.


Japanese importers of Indonesian wood pellets include Mizuho Farm Co., Ltd., Shammah Co., Ltd., SMB Kenzai Co., Ltd., Hanwa Co., Ltd., Optimum Trading Co., Ltd., and CNS Co., Ltd.

1.3 Market Projections and Emerging Trends

By 2025, Japan's wood pellet market reached a valuation of approximately USD 672 million, with a projected compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6.4% through 2034, potentially reaching USD 1.17 billion. Combined Japanese and South Korean industrial pellet imports are projected to exceed 8 million tons annually by 2026.


A notable technological development is the growing demand for torrefied "black pellets" — heat-treated pellets with higher calorific value and hydrophobic properties. These enable outdoor storage, higher co-firing ratios (up to 100% in some applications), and compatibility with existing coal milling infrastructure. While black pellets command a price premium of approximately double that of standard white pellets, they present a significant opportunity for Indonesian producers with access to torrefaction technology.

2.2 SVLK — Timber Legality and Sustainability Certification

The Sistem Verifikasi Legalitas Kayu (SVLK) — Indonesia's Timber Legality and Sustainability Verification System — is the cornerstone of Indonesian wood export regulation. Under Ministry of Environment and Forestry Decision No. SK 9895/MenLHK-PHL/BPPHH/HPL.3/2022, SVLK certification is mandatory for all wood product exporters regardless of destination.


SVLK comprises two core standards:

•       VLK (Verifikasi Legalitas Kayu) — Timber Legality Verification

•       PHPL (Pengelolaan Hutan Produksi Lestari) — Sustainable Production Forest Management


Key operational requirements under SVLK include:

•       An S-LK (Sertifikat Legalitas Kayu) or S-PHPL certificate from a KAN-accredited Certification Body (LVLK) — valid for 3 years with annual surveillance audits

•       A V-Legal Document issued per shipment (per invoice) from an accredited LPVI — valid for 4 months from issuance

•       Full chain-of-custody documentation from raw material source to finished product


Indonesia is the only country globally to hold a Forest Law Enforcement, Governance and Trade (FLEGT) licence. For exports to Japan, the document is referred to as the V-Legal Document. FLEGT licence terminology applies exclusively to EU and UK destinations.

 

2.3 Required Business Licences

Indonesian companies exporting wood pellets must hold the following current licences and registrations:

•       NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha) — Business Identification Number

•       SIUP — Trade Business Licence

•       ETPIK — Registered Exporter of Forest Industry Products (Eksportir Terdaftar Produk Industri Kehutanan)

•       Tax Identification Number (NPWP)

•       Deed of Incorporation and Company Registration Certificate

2.4 Export Licensing Updates: Permendag 8 & 9 of 2025

The Indonesian Ministry of Trade issued updated export policy regulations in 2025 (Permendag 8/2025 and 9/2025), placing forestry products — including wood and bamboo — under enhanced export policy oversight. Exporters are advised to maintain awareness of the current regulated and restricted commodity list under MOF Decree No. 6/KM.4/2025, effective March 2025.

2.5 HS Code Classification

Wood pellets are classified under HS Code 4401.31.00 in Indonesia's customs system (wood pellets: sawdust and wood waste, agglomerated in the form of pellets or similar forms). They are subject to SVLK requirements as a processed wood product originating from the forestry industry. The corresponding Japanese import HS code is 4401.31; the precise 9-digit tariff line should be verified with Japan Customs.

Part 3: Japanese Import Requirements

3.1 Customs Import Procedures

Wood pellets imported into Japan must be declared to Japan Customs (税関) with the correct HS code classification, accompanied by a Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Bill of Lading. Importers must also comply with Japan's Plant Protection Law, which may require quarantine inspection by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF). 

3.2 Plant Quarantine Requirements

Wood pellets, as processed wood products, generally present a lower phytosanitary risk than raw logs or chips. Nevertheless, importers and exporters should observe the following:

•       Confirm with MAFF whether a Phytosanitary Certificate is required for each shipment, based on the processing method applied

•       Pellets manufactured at temperatures exceeding 70°C during the pelletising process are generally considered to have met heat treatment requirements sufficient to eliminate plant pests

•       Consult MAFF's current import permit requirements before each consignment

3.3 Preferential Tariff Rates under JIEPA and AJCEP

Indonesian wood pellets may qualify for preferential tariff rates under the Japan-Indonesia Economic Partnership Agreement (JIEPA, effective July 2008) and the ASEAN-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (AJCEP, effective December 2008). To claim preferential rates, the importer must submit a Certificate of Origin (Form JIEPA or Form AJ) at the time of customs clearance.


Exporters are advised to verify current applicable tariff rates via Japan's METI Tariff Schedule or the Japan Customs Tariff Portal, as rates are subject to periodic revision.

3.4 The Revised Clean Wood Act (Effective 1 April 2025)

Japan's original Clean Wood Act (2016) was a voluntary framework. The Revised Clean Wood Act, passed by the Diet on 8 May 2023, came into full mandatory effect on 1 April 2025 and introduces significant obligations for Type 1 wood-related businesses, including importers.


Under the Revised Act, Japanese importers must:

•       Collect raw material information for each shipment, including: tree species (scientific name preferred), country and region of harvest, and documentation evidencing legality of harvest

•       Conduct risk-based legality due diligence, with heightened checks for high-risk origins

•       Maintain all records for a minimum of 5 years

•       Transfer legality documentation and raw material information to downstream buyers upon sale

•       Report annually where handling volumes exceed 30,000 m³ log equivalent (imported timber) or 15,000 tons (imported furniture, paper, and other goods)

 

Indonesia's SVLK/V-Legal system is explicitly recognised as sufficient documentation under the Revised Clean Wood Act. Exporters should proactively supply V-Legal Documents, species names, and harvest location data with every shipment to facilitate buyer compliance.

While SVLK/V-Legal satisfies Indonesia's mandatory export legality requirement, Japanese power plant operators increasingly require FSC, SBP, or PEFC certification to meet their internal sustainability procurement policies and METI reporting obligations. Exporters targeting FIT/FIP-eligible buyers are strongly advised to pursue one of these certifications.

4.2 Sustainable Biomass Program (SBP)

The SBP certification scheme was specifically approved by METI as compliant with FIT/FIP legislative requirements. For exporters supplying FIT/FIP biomass power plants, SBP certification:

•       Covers the full supply chain from forest source to power plant

•       Requires lifecycle greenhouse gas calculations (see Section 5.2)

•       Provides Japan-specific GHG default values for Indonesian-origin wood pellets based on transport distance: approximately 6,500 km, 9,000 km, and 18,000 km from Indonesia to Japan (per SBP Instruction Document for Japan, v1.0, March 2024)

4.3 METI Biomass Sustainability Criteria

Japan's Biomass Sustainability Working Group evaluates imported biomass fuel under four core criteria:

•       Environment: No clearing of primary forests; protection of biodiversity

•       Social and Labour: Respect for community rights and worker welfare

•       Food Conflicts: No adverse competition with food production

•       Governance: Legal compliance in the country of origin

Part 5: Japan's FIT/FIP Programme and Biomass Eligibility

5.1 Overview of the FIT and FIP Schemes

Japan's Feed-in Tariff (FIT) programme, established under the Act on Special Measures Concerning Procurement of Electricity from Renewable Energy Sources (Act No. 108, 2011), provides guaranteed purchase prices for renewable electricity generated from qualifying biomass power plants.


Since 2022, no new FIT certification has been issued for imported woody biomass power plants. However, existing FIT-certified facilities continue to operate and procure wood pellets, representing the majority of current import demand. The Feed-in Premium (FIP) programme has partially replaced FIT for newer projects, offering market-linked premiums rather than fixed tariffs. For fiscal year 2025–2026, the FIT purchase price for small biomass power projects (under 10 MW) was maintained at ¥24/kWh (approximately USD 160/MWh).

5.2 Lifecycle GHG Emission Requirements

From April 2023, METI required all FIT/FIP biomass power generators to calculate lifecycle GHG emissions for their fuel. METI has published default GHG emission values for different feedstocks and transport routes. Exporters supplying FIT/FIP-eligible power plants must either provide actual lifecycle GHG data or rely on METI-published default values.


The lifecycle calculation must account for the following stages:

•       Forest harvesting

•       Transport to pellet mill

•       Pelletising process, including drying fuel type

•       Maritime transport (using Handy Size or Supramax vessel defaults)

•       Power generation process


Transport distance categories applicable to Indonesian wood pellets (per SBP Instruction Document for Japan, 2024):

•       Short haul: approximately 6,500 km

•       Medium haul: approximately 9,000 km

•       Long haul: approximately 18,000 km

5.3 Mandatory Sustainability Reporting (From April 2024)

From April 2024, METI introduced mandatory sustainability requirements initially targeting Palm Kernel Shell (PKS) imports. Power plants are required to report biomass sustainability information to METI. This measure signals Japan's progressive direction toward mandatory sustainability requirements for all imported biomass, including wood pellets. Indonesian exporters are advised to prepare accordingly.

6.2 ENplus A1 Certification

ENplus A1 is widely regarded as the premium quality standard for wood pellets and is increasingly expected by sophisticated Japanese buyers. It guarantees minimum calorific value of 16.5 MJ/kg, maximum ash content of 0.7%, and maximum moisture content of 10%. Exporters supplying the premium tier of the Japanese market should treat ENplus A1 certification as a material differentiator, with research indicating that certified suppliers can command a price premium of 15–20% over non-certified equivalents.

6.3 Certificate of Analysis (CoA) Requirements

Japanese buyers require a Certificate of Analysis from an accredited testing laboratory for each shipment. The CoA must address, at minimum:

•       Moisture content

•       Ash content

•       Gross and net calorific value

•       Mechanical durability

•       Fines content

•       Bulk density

•       Chemical analysis (chlorine and sulphur content where specified)

6.4 JAS Standard for Wood Pellet Fuel

Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) published a new Japanese Agricultural Standard (JAS) for wood pellet fuel on 15 June 2023. This standard primarily governs residential pellets. For industrial and power generation applications, buyers typically reference ISO 17225-2 or specify quality requirements directly in commercial contracts.

Part 7: Logistics and Shipping

7.1 Principal Export Ports and Shipping Routes

Major Indonesian export ports for wood pellets include Semarang (Central Java), Surabaya (East Java), and Gorontalo (Sulawesi). Typical shipping distances from Indonesian ports to Japanese ports range from 5,000 to 6,500 kilometres.

7.2 IMSBC Code Classification

Wood pellets are classified as a Group B cargo under the International Maritime Solid Bulk Cargoes (IMSBC) Code, indicating that they may liquefy or undergo physical change under certain conditions. Key shipping requirements include:

•       Formal declaration of cargo as wood pellets in accordance with the IMSBC Schedule

•       Shipper's Declaration confirming cargo properties prior to loading

•       Continuous monitoring for carbon monoxide (CO) and oxygen-depleting gases during transport and storage (wood pellets emit CO and CO₂ during stowage)

•       Compliance with applicable ventilation requirements for the cargo hold


Default vessel sizes used in METI's GHG calculations are Handy Size (15,000–35,000 DWT) and Supramax (45,000–60,000 DWT).

7.3 Moisture Control During Transport

Maintaining moisture content at or below 10% throughout the supply chain is critical to product quality and compliance. Exporters should:

•       Utilise covered storage facilities at port

•       Apply moisture-resistant covers or packaging during loading operations

•       Record moisture content in the Certificate of Analysis at the point of export

7.4 Trade Terms and Minimum Order Quantities

Exporters should be prepared to offer both FOB (Free on Board, from Indonesian ports) and CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight, to Japanese destination ports such as Kobe, Yokohama, and Osaka) pricing. Minimum order quantities for container shipments typically commence at 300–500 metric tons, scaling to full Panamax or Supramax vessel cargoes (50,000 metric tons and above) for large utility supply contracts.

Part 8: Compliance Checklist

8.1 Company-Level Requirements (One-Time or Periodic)

•       SVLK S-LK Certificate obtained from a KAN-accredited LVLK (valid 3 years; annual surveillance required)

•       ETPIK registration as a Registered Exporter of Forest Industry Products

•       NIB, SIUP, NPWP, and Company Registration in current order

•       FSC, PEFC, or SBP Chain-of-Custody certification (strongly recommended for FIT/FIP-eligible buyers)

•       Documented supply chain traceability system from raw material source to finished product

8.2 Per-Shipment Documents

•       V-Legal Document (valid 4 months, issued per invoice by accredited LPVI)

•       Certificate of Origin — Form JIEPA or Form AJ (for preferential tariff eligibility)

•       Certificate of Analysis from accredited laboratory

•       Shipper's Declaration / IMSBC cargo declaration

•       Commercial Invoice

•       Packing List

•       Bill of Lading

•       Phytosanitary Certificate (as required by MAFF)

8.3 FIT/FIP Sustainability Compliance

•       SBP, FSC, PEFC, or RSB Japan FIT Scheme certification in place

•       Lifecycle GHG calculation prepared, or METI default values documented

•       Species name and harvest area documentation provided with each shipment (Revised Clean Wood Act)

•       Sustainability criteria compliance confirmed: no primary forest clearing; social and labour standards met

8.4 Japanese Importer Obligations (Post-April 2025 Revised CWA)

•       Raw material information collected from Indonesian supplier (species, harvest area, legality documentation)

•       Legality confirmed via V-Legal Document, FSC CoC, or PEFC CoC

•       Risk-based due diligence conducted and documented

•       Records maintained for a minimum of 5 years

•       Legality and raw material information transferred to downstream buyers

•       Annual report submitted where handling volumes exceed regulatory thresholds

Conclusion

The Japanese market presents a strategically significant and structurally supported opportunity for Indonesian wood pellet exporters. Policy commitments to carbon neutrality, sustained FIT/FIP demand from existing biomass power facilities, and the growing penetration of co-firing technology collectively underpin a long-term, high-volume import requirement that Indonesian producers are geographically and commercially positioned to serve.


Success in this market, however, is contingent upon full regulatory compliance. The Revised Clean Wood Act, mandatory lifecycle GHG reporting, and evolving METI sustainability requirements have raised the compliance threshold for all participants in the supply chain. Indonesian exporters who invest in robust SVLK documentation, pursue FSC, SBP, or PEFC certification, and maintain consistent product quality in accordance with ISO 17225-2 and ENplus A1 standards will be best positioned to secure long-term supply agreements with Japanese utilities and industrial buyers.


Exporters are encouraged to engage proactively with their Japanese counterparts to understand evolving buyer requirements, participate in APREBI and Ministry of Trade trade missions, and consider the strategic value of developing torrefied pellet capacity to access the emerging premium market segment.

 

This guide is intended for informational purposes and reflects regulations and market conditions as of 2025. Exporters and importers should seek independent legal and regulatory advice and verify current requirements with the relevant authorities prior to each transaction.
